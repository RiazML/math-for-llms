# Contamination and Dedup Audits

> _"A benchmark score is only evidence if the benchmark was not in the training data."_

## Overview

Deduplication and contamination audits reduce wasted compute, memorization, privacy
risk, and evaluation leakage. In an LLM training run, data is not an inert pile of text;
it is the empirical distribution that defines the examples, losses, risks, and
capabilities the model will see.

This section is written as LaTeX Markdown. Inline mathematics uses `$...$`, and display
equations use `$$...$$`. The goal is to connect data engineering decisions to
mathematical objects such as records $r_i$, token sequences $x_{1:T}$, filters $f(x)$,
hashes $h(x)$, mixture weights $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$, and empirical expectations.

The scope is deliberately narrow: this chapter owns the training-data pipeline.
Tokenizer design, GPU training systems, benchmark methodology, alignment objectives, and
production MLOps each have their own canonical chapters. Here we study the data objects
that those later systems consume.

## 1. Intuition

Intuition gives the conceptual and mathematical layer for contamination and dedup
audits. The local variables in this section should be read as pipeline objects:
documents, records, tokens, filters, weights, shards, and manifests.

### 1.1 Duplicates waste compute

Duplicates waste compute is part of the canonical scope of contamination and dedup
audits. We model the relevant object as a finite collection $\mathcal{D} =
\{r_i\}_{i=1}^n$ with record-level metadata $m_i$ and text or token content $x_i$. The
practical question is whether the transformation preserves the intended empirical
distribution.

A useful local invariant is:

$$
\text{valid}(r_i, \mathcal{S}) = 1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad r_i \text{ can be consumed by the next pipeline stage.}
$$

For `duplicate`, the invariant should be explicit enough that a checker can fail fast.
If the invariant is only written in a notebook comment or an engineer's memory, it will
not protect a long-running data build.

Examples:
- A small local experiment can store this object in memory; a frontier-scale run must store it as sharded, versioned, validated records.
- The mathematical object is simple, but the operational contract must survive restarts, parallel workers, schema changes, and audits.
- The notebook for this section uses synthetic data so the same ideas can be executed without external files.

Non-examples:
- A path on disk without a manifest is not a reproducible dataset.
- A metric dashboard without record-level lineage is not a provenance system.
- A filter threshold without an audit sample is not evidence of quality.

Implementation consequence: every transformation should report both a count and a rate.
If $n_{\mathrm{in}}$ records enter the stage and $n_{\mathrm{out}}$ records leave, the
acceptance rate is

$$
a = \frac{n_{\mathrm{out}}}{n_{\mathrm{in}}}.
$$

A sudden change in $a$ is a data-drift signal even when the code still runs. This is why
pipeline math is inseparable from logging, manifests, and audit slices.

For LLM work, the token-weighted view is often more important than the document-weighted
view. A filter that removes 5 percent of documents may remove 30 percent of tokens if it
targets long documents. The corresponding token acceptance rate is

$$
a_{\mathrm{tok}} = \frac{\sum_i f(r_i)\,T_i}{\sum_i T_i},
$$

where $T_i$ is the token count or a deterministic token-count estimate. The distinction
matters for compute budgets, mixture proportions, and scaling-law interpretation.

| # | Mistake | Why It Is Wrong | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 11 | Assuming licenses are uniform | Source terms can conflict | Track license at source and record level |

## 11. Why Duplicates Distort Training

Exact duplicates multiply gradient weight on whatever they contain: memorized text, boilerplate, or one source's quirks (Sections 3, 6).
Lee et al. (2022, Deduplicating Training Data Makes Language Models Better) showed deduplication improves training efficiency and reduces verbatim memorization/regurgitation in their experiments.
Duplicates also corrupt evaluation when copies straddle train/holdout splits (Section 04's hash-split discipline exists largely to prevent this).
Near-duplicates are the harder mass: updated pages, templated documents, and quoted copies that exact hashing misses.
The audit question is quantitative: what fraction of tokens are exact duplicates, near-duplicates at a stated threshold, and split-crossing duplicates?

## 12. Exact Deduplication Mechanics

Normalize, hash, group, decide: the pipeline of Section 3 applied at corpus scale.
Normalization decides what 'exact' means — whitespace, case, Unicode form — and must match the format contract of Section 01.
Grouping by full-document hash is cheap and streaming-friendly with external sorting or hash partitioning.
The keep-policy (first, longest, highest-quality, random-seeded) is a decision with mixture consequences and belongs in the audit report.
Removed copies are counted per source; a source that is 80% duplicate of another is a mixture fact, not just a dedup statistic.

## 13. Fuzzy Deduplication: Shingles, MinHash, and Thresholds

Shingling turns documents into token n-gram sets; Jaccard similarity over those sets defines near-duplication (Section 4).
MinHash sketching estimates Jaccard similarity from compact signatures, and LSH banding finds candidate pairs without all-pairs comparison.
Threshold choice is empirical: sample pairs across similarity bands and label them, then pick the band where 'same content, different wrapper' ends.
Worked reading: two pages sharing a template but differing in body may reach 0.5 Jaccard on 5-gram shingles; true copies with light edits sit far higher — the labelled bands make this precise for your corpus.
Clustering near-duplicates (connected components) can over-merge through chains; audit the largest clusters by eye.

## 14. Semantic Deduplication and Its Limits

Embedding-based deduplication (for example SemDeDup, Abbas et al., 2023) clusters documents by meaning and prunes within clusters.
It catches paraphrase-level redundancy hashes cannot see, at the price of embedding cost and a similarity notion inherited from the encoder.
Semantic pruning can remove legitimately distinct documents that merely discuss the same topic; per-cluster keep rates need slice audits (Section 03).
Treat semantic dedup as a filter with precision/recall (Section 03's vocabulary), not as a definition of duplication.

## 15. Benchmark Contamination Audits

Contamination is overlap between training data and evaluation items: questions, passages, or near-paraphrases of them (Section 5).
The audit matches eval items against the corpus with the same machinery as dedup: exact n-gram overlap, fuzzy document match, and embedding neighbours for inspection.
Report contamination per benchmark: number of items with matches, match type, and the action taken (remove training copies, flag the benchmark score, or both).
Dodge et al. (2021) documented overlap analyses for C4 against downstream tasks, an early template for reporting rather than asserting cleanliness.
A score on a contaminated benchmark measures memorization partly; the audit report is what lets readers discount it correctly.
Decontamination removes training-side copies; it cannot retroactively make a published score clean, which is why audits precede training.

## 16. Memorization and Privacy Audits

Memorization risk concentrates where duplicates concentrate: repeated strings are what models reproduce (Section 6).
Canary and extraction audits measure whether specific sequences can be elicited; their design and ethics belong with governance (Section 06).
PII-bearing duplicates multiply exposure: dedup is therefore also a privacy control, complementing Section 03's PII filters.
Removal requests execute against content hashes (Section 01): find every copy, including dedup survivors' siblings, and verify removal in the next build.

## 18. Step-by-Step Worked Problems


**Problem 1: Price exact duplicates.**
- 12% of tokens are exact duplicate copies.
- Removing them shrinks the build and every epoch plan with it (Section 04).
- Mixture shares must be recomputed post-dedup.

**Problem 2: Set a fuzzy threshold.**
- Labelled bands show same-content pairs dominate above 0.85 Jaccard and template-only pairs below 0.7.
- Set the threshold in the labelled gap and record the labelling evidence (Section 13).

**Problem 3: Audit a benchmark.**
- 3% of a public benchmark's items have fuzzy matches in training data.
- Report the matches and remove training copies before any training run uses the build (Section 15).
- Scores already produced elsewhere are not yours to clean; yours must be clean going forward.

**Problem 4: Trace a removal request.**
- A document must be removed from future builds.
- Content hash finds exact copies; fuzzy clusters find edited copies (Sections 12-13).
- Verify absence in the next build's audit, not by intention.

**Problem 5: Challenge a giant cluster.**
- One near-duplicate cluster holds 2M documents.
- Chain merging is the suspect (Section 13).
- Inspect members; tighten banding or split the cluster before deciding keeps.

**Problem 6: Deduplicate code carefully.**
- Code files share licences and templates legitimately.
- Aggressive fuzzy dedup can erase real diversity; use source-aware thresholds and slice audits.

## 22. Worked Contamination Audit (numbered protocol)

1. Freeze the evaluation item sets and the corpus build under audit
2. Compute exact n-gram overlap per benchmark and report counts
3. Run fuzzy document matching for near-copies and sample-inspect matches
4. Inspect embedding neighbours for paraphrase-level matches on a sample
5. Classify each match: train-side copy, eval-side variant, or coincidence
6. Remove train-side copies and record the removal manifest
7. Rebuild or patch the dataset version with a new version string
8. Re-run the audit on the new build to verify removals
9. Publish the per-benchmark table with the dataset documentation
10. Flag any already-produced scores that used the contaminated build

## 23. Choosing What Survives a Duplicate Group

The survivor choice is a quality decision disguised as bookkeeping.
Keeping the longest version favours completeness; keeping the highest quality-scored version imports Section 03's definitions.
Keeping the earliest crawl preserves provenance simplicity; random-seeded choice is defensible when versions are equivalent.
Whatever the rule, it applies uniformly and is stated in the audit report, because survivor choice shifts source shares.

## 24. Dedup Interactions With Mixture and Quality

Deduplication changes every source's token count, so mixture weights computed pre-dedup describe a corpus that no longer exists (Section 07).
Quality filters and dedup interact: a repeated low-quality page is both a filter target and a duplicate multiplier.
Order the stages deliberately and report counts after each, in the cascade accounting style of Section 03.

## 25. Auditing at Scale Without Reading Everything

Stratified samples carry the audit: by source, cluster size, similarity band, and benchmark match type.
Extreme strata (largest clusters, highest overlaps) get exhaustive inspection; the long tail gets sampled rates with confidence intervals.
An audit that reports only global rates hides exactly the strata where harm concentrates.

## 26. Final Problems and Answers

1. **A benchmark's questions appear verbatim in a training source.** Exact contamination: remove train-side copies, report counts, flag prior scores (Section 15).
2. **Near-duplicate clusters merge an entire documentation site.** Chaining over-merge; inspect and re-band before pruning (Section 13).
3. **Dedup removes 30% of one source and 2% of others.** Mixture must be recomputed; the dedup was also a mixture intervention (Section 24).
4. **A removal request names a document that was deduplicated away.** The survivor and its cluster siblings still need checking; dedup is not removal (Section 16).
5. **Semantic dedup prunes most documents about a niche topic.** Encoder similarity collapsed a topic; slice-audit before accepting (Section 14).

## 27. Section 05 Takeaways

- Duplicates are gradient multipliers and memorization fuel; count them in tokens.
- Thresholds come from labelled bands, not folklore.
- Contamination is reported per benchmark with actions, before training.
- Dedup, quality, and mixture are one accounting system.
- Removals are verified in the next build, not asserted.
